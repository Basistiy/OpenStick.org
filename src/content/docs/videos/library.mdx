## 2. Qualcomm EDL Mode, Safety & Unbricking Guides

### Never Brick Your $15 Pi Zero Alternative — EDL Mode Hack

The number one rule of OpenStick: **always back up your stock firmware and radio partitions before touching fastboot or Linux!**

- By holding the reset pin or bridging the test points while plugging into USB, the board boots straight into Qualcomm Emergency Download (EDL / 9008) mode.
- Using the open-source `edl` tool on macOS or Linux, you can dump every original partition to your drive.
- Even if you completely brick the bootloader later, EDL mode lets you restore it back to factory fresh in seconds.

### Unbrick a $20 Snapdragon 410 Modem with Tweezers & Firehose

What happens if you soft-brick your modem while flashing Linux? Here is how to force Qualcomm EDL mode and revive your device to run DOOM:

1. Remove the battery to completely power cycle the SoC.
2. Connect external 3.7V power (or an 18650 cell) to the battery terminal pads.
3. Plug in the USB data cable to your PC.
4. Locate the EDL boot pins on the PCB (the `FB` test point and adjacent pin).
5. Bridge the test pins using metal tweezers while powering on.
6. The modem enters Qualcomm Emergency Download (EDL) Mode (`HS-USB QDLoader 9008`).
7. Push the firehose programmer via `qdl` / `edl.py` and restore your boot partitions!

## 3. Hardware Modifications & Cyberdeck Builds

### Linux on €20 4G Modem with Screen: Adding USB Host Mode & 5V Booster

Upgrading a cheap €20 4G LTE USB modem powered by Qualcomm Snapdragon 410 (MSM8916) into a fully portable, battery-powered Linux computer using OpenStick:

- **The Problem**: While the MSM8916 chipset supports USB host mode, the stock board lacks a 5V power line on the USB port (VBUS).
- **The Mod**: Adding an **MH-CD42 step-up boost converter** to supply 5V to the USB VBUS rail directly from the 3.7V battery.
- **Power Switch**: Installing a power toggle switch on the 5V booster output to prevent current collision between the charger and the booster.
- **Result**: Boots full desktop Linux (Debian ARM64) on battery power, supports external displays, and allows connecting USB keyboards, gaming peripherals to play DOOM, and even a DJI Mic receiver to interact with AI agents.
